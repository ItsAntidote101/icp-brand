# Competitor copy teardown

Read 24 November 2026. Wording is paraphrase unless in quotation marks. Figures are the sites' own unverified claims.

**Could not read:** WordStream's own grader page (fetch failed), so I read the same grader on sister brand LocaliQ. Hey Digital's root home page; I read its /reviews page (laid out like a home page) and its self-written review post. Optmyzr and Kenzobe were read but only noted in passing.

Key: **H** hero and first button. **P** problem. **Pr** proof. **Hu** human shown. **Pg** page types. **B** brave or odd.

## Ad audit and management software

**1. Adzooma.** H: a row of benefit nouns (performance, visibility, control); button "Start Free". P: PPC is scattered across platforms and run on guesses. Pr: counters for reports run and spend analysed, star rating, partner badges, one customer story. Hu: none. Pg: feature pages, tactic pages, role pages, blog, free account reports, one comparison article. B: nothing. Footer notes are years old.

**2. WordStream grader (via LocaliQ).** H: asks if you are wasting money on Google Ads; button "Try it free". P: wasted spend, weak quality scores, neglected accounts. Pr: counters, partner badge, one testimonial. Hu: none. Pg: grader, blog, guides, industry pages. B: needs an email plus a Google sign-in to the ad account, then returns a score and grades. Headline and FAQ figures disagree.

**3. Opteo.** H: coins an acronym for an AI marketing manager; button "Start Free Trial". P: manual work across platforms, tracking that breaks quietly. Pr: review rating, three testimonials from big brand staff, no result numbers. Hu: none. Pg: features, customers, pricing, changelog, glossary. B: a public changelog, and a plain note on why the trial asks for a card. Optmyzr reads the same, with more counters and awards.

**4. Madgicx.** H: dominate with AI ads; button "Unlock the Madgicx". P: owners drown in Ads Manager data. Pr: advertiser count, review site ratings, partner logos, short testimonials. Hu: none. Pg: features, academy, case studies, free tools, glossary, comparisons, benchmarks, public roadmap. B: a crude pun headline, and links that feed AI chatbots a flattering prompt about the product.

## Performance agencies

**5. KlientBoost.** H: an agency judged on client goals hit; button "Get Your Free Marketing Plan". P: your current agency misses its targets. Pr: a goals hit figure, logos, G2 and Clutch badges, case studies as PDFs, a forecast grid of projected revenue. Hu: departments, no names. Pg: channel services, sector pages, pricing, results, team, blog, own software. B: swearing, comedy videos, a promise to drop services that underperform, a public checklist of fixes that invites additions.

**6. Hey Digital.** H: paid media for B2B software firms and nothing else; button "Book a call". P: ads reach the wrong accounts; a generalist agency treated you like a shop. Pr: client count, logos, testimonial cards that each link to a case study. Hu: clients named, own staff on other pages. Pg: services, channels, sectors, results, blog, podcast, ad examples, reviews. B: lists who it is not for, including a minimum budget.

**7. Linear.** H: its own AI finds winning campaigns; button "Free Proposal". P: manual optimisation is slow. Pr: famous logos, a case study grid, headlines built as metric plus timeframe with a client quote. Hu: one named account manager. Pg: services, team, case studies, resources, blog. B: an AI suggestion mock-up in the hero; claims to beat the best human specialists.

**8. Disruptive Advertising.** H: most marketing spend is wasted and it is not your fault, with a 90 day guarantee for qualifying brands; button offers a free marketing audit. P: the leak is rarely the ads, it is how marketing was set up. Pr: Clutch rating, headcount, spend managed, logo wall, awards. Figures differ between sections. Hu: client portraits, office photo. Pg: services, sector pages, values, case studies, reviews, webinars. B: says it turns most inquiries away; limited client slots.

**9. Ladder.** H: growth without guesswork; button offers a free growth review. P: marketing teams cannot keep up, agencies lock you into fixed scopes. Pr: famous logos, metric cards, press logos, one testimonial. Hu: none on the home page. Pg: solutions, case studies, benchmark resources, audit handbook, team, blog. B: an unsourced near total percentage in a headline.

## One person at the centre

**10. Designjoy.** H: a design subscription you can pause or cancel; button "Start today". P: freelancers are unreliable, agencies cost too much. Pr: a celebrity testimonial, logos, product awards, turnaround time. Hu: founder named, says he runs it alone. Pg: one page with steps, scope, price card, long FAQ. B: one public price, pause rules shown with a worked example, a list of what is out of scope. Refund terms contradict each other.

**11. Seb Crivelli, fractional head of paid media.** H: a senior operator hired part time; button books a 30 minute discovery call. P: you need senior paid media thinking without a full time hire. Pr: spend managed, named past employers, specific results, three case study PDFs. Hu: first person, his name is the brand. Pg: one page, downloads, a playbook article. B: open monthly price ranges, blunt principles, a promise of no pitch on the call.

## Kenyan agencies

**12. Nairobi Marketing.** H: your agency in Nairobi for more qualified enquiries; button "Let's discuss your business". P: buyers search across Google, YouTube, social and AI; weak results are often an offer or landing page issue. Pr: Google rating, logo strip, review cards tagged by service, challenge then result case studies. Hu: founder named as author, stock style photos. Pg: services, sector pages, location pages, case studies, guides. B: publishes KES fees (Google Ads from 55,000), separates media spend from fee, says Google Ads is not a fit without someone to follow up enquiries, warns that a case study is a single job and no benchmark. WhatsApp button. No M-Pesa.

**13. BrandBoost Digital.** H: maximise results with a leading Nairobi Google Ads agency; button "GET A QUOTE". P: competitors will outrank you. Pr: none, only a lead guarantee and a claim of results in about a week. Hu: none. Pg: services, city pages, blog, FAQ. B: keyword stuffing.

**14. Akus.** H: qualified leads from Google and YouTube ads in 45 days; the form button is "Submit". P: ads are costly and unpredictable without experts. Pr: a count of accounts handled, no names. Hu: none. Pg: services, packages, our work, blog. B: price in the FAQ (Ksh 35,000 a month or 12 percent of spend). The FAQ says 90 days, the hero says 45. Kenzobe is similar: packages from Ksh 50,000 to 150,000 plus ad spend.

## The sameness

1. Opening on wasted ad spend.
2. Guesswork against data.
3. Real results, more leads, more sales, better ROAS.
4. Giant counters: spend managed, reports run, accounts connected.
5. Logo walls and partner badges.
6. Star ratings borrowed from G2, Clutch or Google.
7. Percentage lift headlines with no starting figure.
8. A free audit, plan, proposal or strategy that is a sales call.
9. Our team of experts, a dedicated account manager.
10. AI in the hero.
11. Results in 7, 45 or 90 days, with a guarantee.
12. Leading, top, number one, award winning.
13. Blaming other agencies.
14. Service menus by channel, then keyword stuffed city pages.
15. Buttons: start free, book a call, get a quote, submit.

## Gaps nobody fills

- **Spend less first.** Every route ends in more spend or a call. Disruptive says the leak is rarely the ads, then sells a 90 day programme.
- **After the inquiry.** Graders score the account. Nobody checks reply speed, follow up or who is asking. Nairobi Marketing mentions follow up only as a condition of fit.
- **No login, no call.** The grader wants account access. Agency audits want a meeting. Ideal ICP needs neither.
- **A healthy verdict.** No site says the answer may be that you are fine.
- **Work handed over.** Software keeps advice inside the app, agencies keep the work. Nobody hands over upload ready files and words to paste.
- **One named person.** Software shows nobody, agencies show departments. Only Designjoy and the fractional consultant do, and neither sells ads help to Kenyan owners.
- **Receipts.** Proof is lifts, logos and stars. Spend, inquiries and buyers with dates is almost unseen.
- **Local money.** Kenyan management fees read start at Ksh 35,000 a month. None mention paying by M-Pesa, month by month with no renewal.
- **Their scenes.** No site describes a WhatsApp chat going quiet.
- **Lists stay on your device.** Nobody says this.

## Borrowable structures

- **Case study.** Hey Digital's best one compares the same months a year apart and reports spend next to closed deals from the CRM. Use: dates, spend, inquiries, buyers, before and after, what changed, plus Nairobi Marketing's warning that one job is no benchmark. Linear's headline shape: number plus timeframe.
- **Service page.** Nairobi Marketing: a summary box on top (who it suits, price), what is done, a not a fit section, a fee table apart from media spend, FAQ on who owns the account.
- **Fit and scope.** Hey Digital's who we are not for list; Designjoy's out of scope list.
- **Pricing.** One card per plan, pause rules with a worked example (Designjoy), a downloadable one page offer menu (Crivelli).
- **Reviews.** Cards tagged by sector, each linked to its matching receipt.
- **Contact.** WhatsApp button beside a short form; state who replies and that no pitch follows.
- **Resources.** Madgicx splits learning into guides, answers, glossary, comparisons. KlientBoost publishes its checklist. Opteo keeps a dated changelog. Use: answers by owner question, a plain glossary, a public list of what the diagnosis checks.
