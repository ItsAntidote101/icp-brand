# Why are my Google Ads not converting?
Usually because the clicks come from searches you never meant to pay for. Google shows you those searches, so read them before you touch the budget.

## prose
### Where do I see what people really typed?
Your keywords are what you chose. Search terms are what people typed before your ad showed. The two can be far apart.

In your Google Ads account, select Campaigns, open Insights and reports, then click Search terms. Pick the last 30 days and read down the list.

Sort each line into buyer or not a buyer. A college advertising a diploma might find "free online courses". A dental clinic might find "dentist jobs Nairobi", and a law firm might find students looking for case notes.

Google leaves out searches that only a few people made, so the list is never complete. It is still the most useful page in the account.

### Why is Google showing my ad for those searches?
Match type. Every keyword is broad match unless you say otherwise. Broad match lets your ad show for searches Google judges to be related, including some that do not mean the same thing.

You tighten it with punctuation. Put the keyword in quotation marks for phrase match, or in square brackets for exact match. Fewer searches will match, and the ones that do sit closer to what you wrote.

### How do I stop paying for the wrong searches?
Block them with negative keywords.

In the Search terms report, tick the box beside each bad search and click Add as negative keyword. Choose ad group, campaign or a negative keyword list, then click Save.

Two details catch owners out:
- A negative added this way is exact match. It blocks that one search and nothing else. "Dentist jobs Nairobi" is gone, while "dental assistant jobs" still gets through. Add the single word "jobs" as a negative keyword as well.
- Negative keywords do not cover plurals or synonyms. Block "job" and "jobs", "course" and "courses". Misspellings are handled for you.

Run this sum on your own report. Say you spent KES 30,000 last month and KES 10,000 of it went on searches from job seekers and students. A third of the budget reached people who were never going to buy. Both figures are made up to show the arithmetic, so put your own in.

### Am I paying for clicks from outside my area?
You might be. Google's default location option is "Presence or interest". It reaches people in the places you chose, plus people who have shown interest in those places, for example by searching for them from somewhere else.

That suits a property agency selling to Kenyans abroad. A clinic in Nakuru wants the other option, "Presence", which keeps to people who are in, or regularly in, your locations. Open the campaign's settings, go to Locations, and check which of the two is selected.

### What if the right people click and still do not buy?
Then the leak is after the click, and no keyword will fix it. Three checks you can do today:
- Open the page your ad points to, on your phone. The promise in the ad should be the first thing you read.
- In Google Ads, go to Goals, then Conversions. If no call, form or WhatsApp action is listed there, Google cannot tell which searches brought inquiries, and neither can you.
- Send your own business an inquiry at 9pm and time the reply. Then count last month's inquiries from Google and how many of them paid.

If plenty asked and few paid, the work is in the reply.

## faq
### Questions owners ask next
#### How often should I read the search terms report?
Every week while a campaign is new. New searches keep arriving, so one clean-up is never the last.
#### Should I raise the budget to get more conversions?
Not yet. More budget buys more of the same searches. Fix what you are paying for first, then decide.
